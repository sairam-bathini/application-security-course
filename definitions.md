# 📚 Cybersecurity Fundamentals Glossary

## Authentication

The process of verifying the identity of a user or system before granting access to resources.

---

## Authorization

The process of determining if a user has permission to access a specific resource after authentication.

---

## Session

A period of interaction between a user and a system, typically secured by authentication.

---

## Cookies

Small pieces of data stored by a web browser to remember user preferences and session information.

---

## Encryption

The process of converting information into a secure format that can only be accessed by authorized parties.

---

## Decryption

The process of converting encrypted data back to its original form.

---

## Firewall

A security system that monitors and controls incoming and outgoing network traffic based on predetermined rules.

---

## Malware

Malicious software designed to harm, exploit, or steal data from systems or networks.

---

## Virus

A type of malware that attaches itself to legitimate programs and spreads to other systems.

---

## Ransomware

A type of malware that locks users out of their systems or data and demands payment to restore access.

---

## Phishing

A social engineering attack where attackers impersonate legitimate entities to steal sensitive information.

---

## Social Engineering

Manipulating people into revealing confidential information or performing actions that compromise security.

---

## Threat

A potential event or action that could cause harm to a system or data.

---

## Vulnerability

A weakness in a system or software that could be exploited by a threat.

---

## Exploit

A technique used to take advantage of a vulnerability in a system.

---

## Zero-Day

A vulnerability that is discovered and exploited before a patch is available.

---

## Patch

An update to software or a system that fixes security vulnerabilities or bugs.

---

## Credentials

Information used to verify identity, such as usernames and passwords.

---

## Access Control

Mechanisms that restrict unauthorized users from accessing specific resources.

---

## Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)

A security method that requires two or more forms of authentication to verify identity.

---

## Hashing

The process of converting data into a fixed-size string of characters that represents the original data.

---

## Man-in-the-Middle (MITM) Attack

An attack where a third party intercepts and potentially alters communications between two parties.

---

## Denial-of-Service (DoS) Attack

An attack that overloads a system or network, making it unavailable to users.

---

## Distributed Denial-of-Service (DDoS) Attack

A more powerful form of DoS attack where multiple systems flood a target and cause service disruption.

---

## Intrusion Detection System (IDS)

A system that monitors network or system activities for malicious activity or policy violations.

---

## Intrusion Prevention System (IPS)

A system that detects and prevents unauthorized access to a network or system.

---

## Virtual Private Network (VPN)

A secure network connection over the internet that encrypts data and hides the user's IP address.

---

## Penetration Testing

Simulated cyberattacks performed to identify and fix vulnerabilities before attackers can exploit them.

---

## Vulnerability Assessment

The process of identifying, classifying, and prioritizing vulnerabilities in a system.

---

## Incident Response

The approach taken to detect, investigate, contain, and recover from security incidents.

---

## Digital Forensics

The process of investigating and analyzing systems to gather evidence after a security incident.

---

## Security Operations Center (SOC)

A centralized team responsible for monitoring, detecting, and responding to security threats.

---

## Security Information and Event Management (SIEM)

A platform that collects, analyzes, correlates, and reports security-related data across an organization.

---

## Brute Force Attack

An attack that uses trial and error to guess passwords, encryption keys, or login credentials.

---

## SQL Injection (SQLi)

An attack that exploits vulnerabilities in an application's database layer by inserting malicious SQL queries.

---

## Cross-Site Scripting (XSS)

An attack that injects malicious scripts into web pages, which are executed by users' browsers.

---

## Cross-Site Request Forgery (CSRF)

An attack that tricks an authenticated user into performing unintended actions on a website.

---

## Content Security Policy (CSP)

A browser security feature that helps prevent XSS and code injection attacks by defining trusted content sources.

---

## Principle of Least Privilege (PoLP)

A security principle that grants users and systems only the minimum access necessary to perform their tasks.

---

## Data Breach

An incident where sensitive, protected, or confidential information is accessed, disclosed, or stolen without authorization.

---

## Security Policies

A set of rules and practices that govern how an organization protects its information and IT systems.

---

## Compliance

Adhering to industry standards, laws, and regulations related to cybersecurity and data protection.

---

## Risk Assessment

The process of identifying, analyzing, evaluating, and prioritizing risks to systems and data.

---

## Patch Management

The process of regularly updating software and systems to fix vulnerabilities and improve security.

---

## Sandboxing

The practice of isolating programs or code in a controlled environment to prevent impacts on other systems.

---

## API Security

The practice of protecting Application Programming Interfaces (APIs) from unauthorized access and attacks.

---

## Cryptography

The science of securing communication and information through encryption, hashing, and related techniques.

---

## Threat Modeling

The process of identifying potential threats, attack vectors, and mitigations during system design.

---

## GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation)

A European Union regulation designed to protect personal data and privacy for individuals within the EU.

---

## NIST (National Institute of Standards and Technology)

A U.S. government agency that develops cybersecurity standards, frameworks, and best practices.

---

## ISO 27001

An international standard for Information Security Management Systems (ISMS) that helps organizations manage and protect information securely.
